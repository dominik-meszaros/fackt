# Writing for fackt.io

## The golden rule

Every blog note and every piece of site copy must be so valuable that after reading a few paragraphs, a founder has no reason not to book a call, and thinks: "wow, that makes sense."

We do not win by sounding impressive. We win by being the clearest, most useful thing the reader has read about their problem this month, so that talking to Dom feels like the obvious next step.

## What every blog note must have

1. **The reader's situation in the first three sentences.** Name the exact moment they are in ("your trial users sign up and never come back") and what it costs them. No warm-up, no definitions they already know.
2. **One "aha".** A single non-obvious insight, stated plainly and early, that changes how they see the problem. Usually: why the common approach fails. If a note has no aha, it is not ready.
3. **A model they can remember.** Name the idea (the first win, the three questions, the five second test) so they can repeat it to their team.
4. **Concrete before and after.** Real headlines, real screens, real decisions. Show the weak version and the strong version side by side. Abstract advice ("keep it simple") is not allowed without an example.
5. **Proof from real Fackt work.** Use what actually happened in a case study (FireCut's 100 cancellation responses, Polar's four message angles, Floxy's pixel identity). Never invent numbers, clients, quotes or results. If a fact is not in a case study or confirmed by Dom, it does not go in.
6. **Something to do today.** A test or exercise that takes under an hour, so the reader sees the gap in their own product with their own eyes.
7. **An honest bridge to the call.** Explain what is hard to see from the inside, and what Dom would look at in their product. The reader should feel the call is the shortcut, not a sales pitch.
8. **Quick answers.** Three short questions and answers at the end (the `faq` field), written as direct, quotable answers.

## Style

- Plain words, short paragraphs, headings that state the value ("Count the steps before it", not "Step two").
- Cut every sentence the reader already knows. Every section has to earn its place.
- Specific beats clever. Numbers only when they are real.
- Never use em dashes or en dashes. Use commas, colons, periods or parentheses.
- Do not claim a senior designer is assigned.
- Pricing: lead with the client's goal; many teams start with a fixed-scope project and move to a retainer later.
- No links in the middle of the opening; link case studies where they prove a point, and end with the audit and the call (the note box does this automatically).

## Site copy

The same rule applies to every page. Each page answers, in this order: what this is, who it is for, why they should believe it, and what to do next. Lead with the visitor's outcome, show the work as proof, and make the next step (free audit or call) obvious and low risk.

## Before publishing, check

- Would a founder forward this to their co-founder? If not, sharpen the aha.
- Could a competitor have written the same note? If yes, add a real example from our work.
- Does the reader know exactly what to do in the next hour?
- Is there a single invented fact? Remove it.
