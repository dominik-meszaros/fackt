challengeTitle: Lab directors trust evidence, not adjectives
challenge: |-
  Diagnostics software is bought by people who check numbers for a living. Most medtech sites give them stock photos of smiling doctors, a vague promise of AI and a demo button.

  The site needed to show what the product actually does on a slide, prove it is accurate, and answer the questions a lab asks before a pilot: which scanners, which systems, which certifications, what it costs.
approachTitle: Put the slide on the screen
approach: |-
  The hero is the microscope view: blood cells drifting under the lens, each one labelled with a class and a confidence score, and one atypical cell flagged for review. The headline only has to say the rest: read every sample, in seconds.

  Every section after it shows the product working. A three step flow from slide to signed report. A cell census that compares 4,812 counted cells with the 100 of a manual count. A review screen where one click opens any detection. An agreement matrix against three pathologists, with the numbers in full.

  Then the practical part, in the order a lab buys: four modules, scanners and lab systems it connects to, the certifications it holds, and pricing per lab instead of per slide.

  We designed it in Figma and built it in Framer, with the microscope animation running live on the page.
