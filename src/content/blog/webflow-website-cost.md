Webflow is the builder a lot of SaaS teams pick when the website is going to grow into a real content machine: a blog, a changelog, customer stories, docs, several languages. That power comes with a slightly more complex bill than Framer's.

**For a typical SaaS, a Webflow website costs $15 to $25 a month for the site plan, plus seats for your team, plus anywhere from about $80 for a template to around $10,000 for a custom landing page designed and built by a studio.** Here is the breakdown.

## 1. The site plan

At the time of writing, Webflow prices each site separately, billed yearly:

| Plan | Price | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | Free | Building and testing, no custom domain |
| Basic | $15 a month | A marketing site with no CMS content, up to 300 pages |
| Premium | $25 a month | Most SaaS sites: CMS with up to 20,000 items for a blog, changelog and case studies |
| Team and Enterprise | From $2,500 a month | Large sites with big teams and contracts |

The jump that matters for a SaaS is from Basic to a plan with the CMS. The moment you want a blog or customer stories your team can publish on their own, you need it. Webflow changes its plans now and then, so check [Webflow's pricing page](https://webflow.com/pricing) before you budget.

## 2. Seats: the cost people forget

Webflow charges for the people who work in your workspace, on top of the site plan. Full seats, for people who design and build, cost $39 a month each, and limited seats for people who mainly edit content cost $15 a month each, billed yearly. Workspace plans for teams and agencies are priced separately.

For a founder editing the site alone, this barely matters. For a marketing team of five, it can cost more than the site plan itself. Plan who really needs to be in Webflow, and who only needs to publish posts.

## 3. A template, or a custom design

**A template** is the cheapest way in. Our own Webflow template, Carefront, sells for $79 on the Webflow Marketplace, and most sit in a similar range. You get a working site with a CMS already set up. You still need to rewrite the copy, swap in your product and adjust the design so it does not look like everyone else who bought it.

**A custom design** starts from your product and your buyer, then gets built in Webflow with components and CMS collections your team can use. That is where the budget goes from under a hundred dollars to thousands.

## 4. Design and build: who does it

The build cost is about the same in Webflow and Framer, because most of the work is the design and the structure, not the tool. For one SaaS landing page, designed and built, these are the ranges from our [website cost calculator](/tools/saas-website-cost-calculator):

| Who | One landing page | Five page marketing site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freelancer | about $2,000 to $6,500 | about $4,000 to $14,000 |
| Specialist studio | about $4,000 to $10,500 | about $8,500 to $23,500 |
| Large agency | about $7,000 to $17,500 | about $14,000 to $39,500 |

Webflow projects tend to sit toward the top of each range when they include a full CMS setup: collection structure, templates for every content type, and a clear way for the team to add new pages. At Fackt, a Webflow landing page starts at $5,000, designed and built, in 2 to 6 weeks.

## 5. When Webflow is worth the higher bill

Webflow is the better buy when:

- **Content is the growth channel.** Many collections, many authors, hundreds of posts or programmatic pages.
- **You need roles and workflows.** Writers who publish, reviewers who approve, developers who own the structure.
- **You publish in several languages** and want localization handled in one place.

For a launch page or a small marketing site, Framer is usually faster to design in and cheaper to run. ## What it costs after launch

- **The site plan and seats**, every year.
- **New pages and sections** as the product changes, booked as small projects or covered by a designer on a monthly plan.
- **Content.** The CMS is only worth it if someone writes. Budget time for that, not just software.
