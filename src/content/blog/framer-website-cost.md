Framer itself is cheap. The plan for a SaaS website costs about the price of two coffees a month. What decides the budget is everything around it: whether you start from a template or a custom design, who builds it, and what you add over time.

**For a typical SaaS, a Framer website costs $10 to $30 a month to run, plus anywhere from $50 for a template to around $10,000 for a custom landing page designed and built by a studio.** Here is where that money goes, with the numbers we see on our own projects.

## 1. The Framer plan

At the time of writing, Framer's site plans are priced per month:

| Plan | Price | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Trying Framer, no custom domain |
| Basic | $10 a month | A launch page or a small site, up to 30 pages and 2 CMS collections |
| Pro | $30 a month | Most SaaS marketing sites: 150 pages, 10 CMS collections, staging |
| Enterprise | Custom | Large sites, many editors |

Most SaaS teams land on Pro once they add a blog, a changelog or customer stories, because each of those is a CMS collection. Prices change, so check [Framer's pricing page](https://www.framer.com/pricing) before you budget.

## 2. Extras that add up

The plan is rarely the whole monthly bill:

- **Editors.** Extra editors are $20 a month each, content-only editors $10. A team of four marketers adds up faster than the plan itself.
- **Languages.** Each extra locale is $20 a month. Translating into five languages costs more than Pro.
- **Bandwidth.** Basic includes 50 GB a month and Pro 100 GB. Heavy video on the homepage is the usual reason to go over.
- **A/B testing.** Available on Pro as a paid add-on, priced by the number of events.

## 3. A template, or a custom design

This is the biggest decision, and the biggest difference in price.

**A template** costs about $50 to $130 on the Framer Marketplace. Our own Framer templates, like Booth and Meridian HQ, sit in that range. A template is a good start for a pre-launch product: you swap the copy, the screenshots and the colors, and you are live in days. The catch is that it was not designed around your product, so the first screen rarely says what you do as clearly as it could.

**A custom design** starts from your product and your buyer, then gets built in Framer. That is where the cost moves from tens to thousands of dollars.

## 4. Design and build: who does it

For a single SaaS landing page, designed and built in Framer, these are the ranges from our [website cost calculator](/tools/saas-website-cost-calculator), which you can adjust for your own scope:

| Who | One landing page | Five page marketing site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freelancer | about $2,000 to $6,500 | about $4,000 to $14,000 |
| Specialist studio | about $4,000 to $10,500 | about $8,500 to $23,500 |
| Large agency | about $7,000 to $17,500 | about $14,000 to $39,500 |

The spread inside each range comes from the extras: copywriting, custom illustration or 3D, motion, a CMS for a blog, or a full brand identity. At Fackt, a Framer landing page starts at $5,000, designed and built, in 2 to 6 weeks.

## 5. What it costs after launch

A Framer site is cheap to keep alive, and that is the point of choosing it. Your team can change headlines, add case studies and publish posts without a developer. What you do keep paying for:

- **The plan**, $10 to $30 a month, plus editors and locales.
- **New pages and sections** as the product grows. Some teams book these as small projects, others keep a designer on a monthly plan.
- **Redirects and SEO care** when pages move. If you are coming from another platform, read [how to move your site to Framer without losing Google rankings](/blog/move-website-to-framer-without-losing-seo).

## What we would do at each stage

- **Before launch, no revenue yet:** a template on Basic. Spend the money on the product.
- **Launching or raising:** a custom landing page on Pro, built around one clear message and real product screens.
- **Growing, with a marketing team:** a custom multi page site with a CMS your team runs, and a designer on call for new pages.
